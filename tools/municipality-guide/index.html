Puerto Rico Municipality Guide

Explore the history of Puerto Rico's 78 municipalities. This interactive tool helps researchers determine record availability, identify parish jurisdictions, and visualize the island's administrative evolution.
Use this guide to bridge the "Record Gap" between civil and church archives.

1885

Civil Registration

Mandatory Civil Registration (Registro Civil) began across the island under Spanish rule. For research prior to this date, Church records are essential.

1931

Centralization

The Department of Health (Demographic Registry) centralized record keeping. Before this, records were held locally in municipalities.

⛪

Parish Gap

Parish records often pre-date civil records by 100+ years. If a town was founded in 1750, the Church is your only primary source until 1885.

Where to Find Records

  • 1.
    FamilySearch: Most comprehensive collection. Look for Registros Parroquiales (Catholic) and Registro Civil.
  • 2.
    Ancestry: Excellent for indexed 1910-1950 Census records and many civil registration books.
  • 3.
    Local Parishes: For records not yet digitized (often post-1900), physical visits or written requests are required.
  • 4.
    Demographic Registry: The official source for certified copies needed for legal purposes.

Research Tip

Always identify the "Mother Town". If your town was founded in 1880, but your ancestor was born in 1850, their birth record will likely be in the municipality that the new town separated from (often noted in the "Notes" section above).
