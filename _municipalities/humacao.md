historical_overview: "Humacao, founded along the southeastern coast in 1722 by Canary Island immigrants, grew into the regional administrative and ecclesiastical hub of eastern Puerto Rico. Known as 'La Perla del Oriente' (The Pearl of the East) and 'La Ciudad Gris' (The Gray City) for its sugar mill smoke, the municipality is the mother town of five neighboring municipalities: Las Piedras, Yabucoa, Juncos, Naguabo, and Vieques. The parish of Dulce Nombre de Jesús holds records from 1784, including racially stratified marriage registers that are essential for Afro-Puerto Rican genealogy research. Researchers seeking ancestors before the parish's establishment should consult the Loíza archives, where residents registered vital events before Humacao had its own resident priest."
